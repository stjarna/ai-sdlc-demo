## Objective

Introduce optional task Due Dates as a new product requirement and deliver the change through the actual AI SDLC Orchestrator.

Demonstrate that AI-assisted engineering can extend an existing product while preserving requirements traceability, governance, human accountability and independent quality assurance.

## 1. Use the installed SDLC skill

Operate from the Littlelist project directory.

Invoke the installed `$sdlc` skill and use its existing conversational workflow.

Begin with a new Discovery request describing the business need:

"Littlelist users should be able to optionally assign a due date to a task, view that date in their task list and manage it when editing a task. Existing tasks without due dates must remain supported."

Let Discovery, Business Refinement and Functional Analysis determine the detailed behavior.

Do not bypass the orchestrator by writing application code directly.

Do not introduce new lifecycle stages or modify the orchestrator.

## 2. Minimize human interaction

Use a low-interruption execution approach.

- Let domain agents independently propose reasonable business, UX and technical solutions.
- Reuse information already established in approved artifacts.
- Avoid repeatedly asking for preferences that do not materially affect the outcome.
- Present concise recommendations whenever a human decision is required.
- Group related questions where the existing workflow permits it.
- Prefer sensible, documented technical choices over unnecessary questions.
- Continue autonomously through operations that do not require explicit human authorization.

Important: Do not fabricate human decisions, identities, approval records or authorization.

Mandatory approval gates must remain genuine.

Do not interpret general trust in the agent as permission to approve future unknown artifact revisions.

## 3. Business requirements

The new feature should support:

- Optional due dates.
- Assigning a due date when creating or editing tasks.
- Displaying assigned dates clearly.
- Changing or removing previously assigned dates.
- Preserving tasks that have no due date.
- Preserving existing CRUD and completion behavior.
- Persisting due dates across application restarts.
- An intuitive and visually polished experience consistent with Littlelist.

Discovery should explicitly address consequential ambiguities, particularly:

- Whether past dates are permitted.
- Whether today's date is permitted.
- How overdue tasks should be represented.
- Whether completed tasks retain due dates.
- Date editing and clearing behavior.
- Validation and calendar-date interpretation.

These are topics for Discovery and human business decisions, not implementation assumptions.

Do not introduce prototype-only limitations merely to avoid resolving business requirements.

Keep the feature appropriately scoped for a local single-user demonstrator.
